# High-flow nasal cannula versus non-invasive positive-pressure ventilation in children with bronchiolitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol

## Review objective

To compare high-flow nasal cannula with non-invasive positive-pressure ventilation in children with bronchiolitis for treatment failure, need for intubation, and pediatric intensive care unit length of stay.

## Review question

In children with bronchiolitis, how does high-flow nasal cannula compare with non-invasive positive-pressure ventilation for treatment failure, need for intubation, and pediatric intensive care unit length of stay?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Children with bronchiolitis.

### Intervention

High-flow nasal cannula.

### Comparator

Non-invasive positive-pressure ventilation, including continuous positive airway pressure and non-invasive positive-pressure ventilation.

### Study design

Published randomized controlled trials reporting at least one eligible outcome. Duplicate publications, reviews, conference abstracts, and case reports will be excluded.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, EMBASE, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science will be searched from database inception through May 2022 without language restrictions. Search concepts will include high-flow nasal cannula, bronchiolitis, children, and randomized trials.

## Study selection

Two reviewers will independently screen records and assess full texts. Disagreements will be referred to a third reviewer.

## Outcomes

- Treatment failure as defined by each trial.
- Need for intubation.
- Pediatric intensive care unit length of stay.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract author, sample size, publication year, study design, participant age and weight, proportion positive for respiratory syncytial virus, baseline heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and eligible outcomes using a standardized form. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus or consultation with a third reviewer.

## Quality assessment

Methodological quality will be assessed independently using the Modified Jadad scale.

## Data synthesis

Analyses will be conducted in Stata 12 using the `metan` command. Dichotomous outcomes will be synthesized as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals and continuous outcomes as weighted mean differences with 95% confidence intervals. Random-effects models will be used for all pooled outcomes. Heterogeneity will be assessed with I²; sensitivity or subgroup analysis will be considered when heterogeneity is high. Two-sided P values below 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

## Reporting-bias assessment

Publication bias will be assessed with funnel plots and Begg's test.
