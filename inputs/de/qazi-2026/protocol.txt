# Tenecteplase for acute ischemic stroke in the 4.5–24-hour treatment window: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol

## Review objectives

To evaluate the efficacy and safety of intravenous tenecteplase in adults with acute ischemic stroke treated 4.5 to 24 hours after last known well; to compare settings in which endovascular thrombectomy is permitted with settings in which it is not available or planned; and to examine whether imaging-selection strategy helps explain variation in outcomes.

## Review question

In adults with acute ischemic stroke treated 4.5 to 24 hours after last known well, how does intravenous tenecteplase compare with placebo or standard medical care for functional and safety outcomes, and do thrombectomy availability or imaging-selection strategy modify those outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Adults aged 18 years or older with acute ischemic stroke presenting 4.5 to 24 hours after last known well and selected using CT or MRI perfusion or DWI-FLAIR mismatch.

### Intervention

Intravenous tenecteplase. Tenecteplase dose will be extracted as a study variable.

### Comparator

Placebo, standard care, or standard medical treatment.

### Study design

Randomized studies. Nonrandomized studies, observational cohorts, and abstracts without sufficient quantitative data will be excluded. Only English-language reports will be included.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Embase, and Scopus will be searched from inception through February 2026. Broad terms for tenecteplase and acute ischemic stroke will be used, and the extended-window criterion will be applied during screening. Forward citation searching of included studies will identify additional records.

## Study selection

Two reviewers will independently screen records and full texts. Disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer.

## Outcomes

### Efficacy outcomes

- Excellent functional outcome, defined as modified Rankin Scale 0 to 1 at 90 days.
- Good functional outcome, defined as modified Rankin Scale 0 to 2 at 90 days.
- Recanalization as defined by each trial.
- Reperfusion as defined by each trial.
- Early neurological improvement, generally defined by a change in National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score within 24 to 72 hours.

Recanalization and reperfusion will be analyzed as surrogate outcomes separately from final functional outcomes.

### Safety outcomes

- Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage.
- Mortality at 90 days.
- Moderate or severe systemic bleeding.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract study characteristics, sample size, participant demographics, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, imaging-selection method, occlusion site, tenecteplase dose, treatment window, comparator, protocol-level thrombectomy permissibility, observed thrombectomy use, and eligible efficacy and safety outcomes. When available, outcomes will be extracted separately for thrombectomy-permitted and non-thrombectomy settings. Disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer. Study authors will not be contacted for additional information.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Two reviewers will independently assess risk of bias using Cochrane RoB 2, with a process to resolve disagreements.

## Data synthesis

Random-effects meta-analysis will be conducted in R 4.4.2 using the `meta` package. Dichotomous outcomes will be summarized as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Between-study variance, τ², will be estimated using restricted maximum likelihood, with Q-profile confidence intervals and Hartung-Knapp adjustment for pooled estimates. Heterogeneity will be assessed with Cochran's Q and I²; values around 25%, 50%, and 75% will be interpreted as low, moderate, and substantial heterogeneity. Two-sided P values below 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

## Subgroup analysis

Trials will be classified by protocol-level access to endovascular thrombectomy rather than observed treatment uptake. Non-thrombectomy settings are those in which thrombectomy is not planned or available; thrombectomy-permitted settings allow thrombectomy or bridging therapy. Differences between subgroups will be tested with chi-square interaction tests and interpreted as exploratory. Imaging-selection strategies will be examined as a potential source of outcome heterogeneity where data permit.

## Sensitivity and diagnostic analyses

Robustness and influence will be examined with leave-one-out analysis, GOSH diagnostics, Baujat plots, and drapery plots. Funnel plots will be used to assess small-study effects where appropriate.

## Certainty of evidence

Certainty of evidence will be assessed using GRADEpro GDT.
