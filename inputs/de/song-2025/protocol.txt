# Early intervention for asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol

## Review objective

To evaluate clinical outcomes of early aortic valve replacement compared with conservative management in adults with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis and to examine surgical and transcatheter intervention separately.

## Review question

In adults with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis, how does early surgical or transcatheter aortic valve replacement compare with conservative management for the prespecified composite and secondary clinical outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Adults aged 18 years or older with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis. Symptomatic patients and participants younger than 18 years will be excluded.

### Intervention

Early surgical aortic valve replacement or transcatheter aortic valve replacement.

### Comparator

Conservative management.

### Study design

Prospective randomized controlled trials with a control arm. Nonrandomized studies, reviews, case series, and studies without a control arm will be excluded.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Library will be searched through November 2024 without restriction by publication year or language. The strategy will follow PICOS concepts for asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis and early surgical or transcatheter valve replacement.

## Study selection

Two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts and assess full texts. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus with a third reviewer.

## Outcomes

### Primary outcome

- Composite of all-cause mortality, hospitalization for cardiovascular causes, stroke, and myocardial infarction.

### Secondary outcomes

- All-cause mortality.
- Hospitalization for cardiovascular causes.
- Stroke.
- Myocardial infarction.
- Cardiac-specific mortality.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract data into a structured Microsoft Excel 2017 form. Fields will include author, year, study design and region, population and sample size, follow-up duration, severity of aortic stenosis, baseline and echocardiographic characteristics, surgical or transcatheter intervention, comparator details, heart-failure or cardiovascular hospitalization where reported, and all eligible outcomes. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion and consensus with a third reviewer.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 1 tool, covering selection, performance, detection, attrition, reporting, and other bias.

## Data synthesis

Dichotomous outcomes will be summarized as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals using a fixed-effect primary model. Heterogeneity will be assessed with Cochran's Q, using P below 0.10 as evidence of heterogeneity, and with I², interpreted as below 25% low, 25% to 50% moderate, and above 50% high heterogeneity. Meta-analysis will be conducted in Review Manager 5.3. The `metafor` package in R 4.3.3 will be used for power analysis of the meta-analysis. Two-sided P values below 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

## Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

Subgroup analysis will compare surgical with transcatheter aortic valve replacement. Sensitivity analyses will include a random-effects REML prediction interval and leave-one-study-out analysis. Univariable random-effects meta-regression will examine age, body mass index, peak aortic jet velocity, and follow-up duration.

## Reporting bias and certainty assessment

Funnel plots will be used to assess publication bias. Certainty of evidence will be assessed using GRADE, with GRADEpro used to prepare evidence profiles.
