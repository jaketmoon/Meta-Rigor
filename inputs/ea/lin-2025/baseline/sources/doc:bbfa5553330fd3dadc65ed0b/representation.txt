THERAPEUTICS

CLINICAL IMPACT RATINGS

In acute stroke with a large ischemic region, endovascular therapy improved functional status at 90 d

Yoshimura S, Sakai N, Yamagami H, et al. Endovascular therapy for acute stroke with a large ischemic region. N Engl J Med. 2022;386:1303-13.

Question: In patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by large-vessel occlusion (LVO) and a large ischemic region, does adding endovascular therapy (EVT) to medical care improve clinical outcomes?

Design: Randomized controlled trial (RESCUE-Japan LIMIT [Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-Acute Embolism -Japan Large Ischemic Core Trial]).

Blinding: Treatment allocation concealed; blinded (outcome assessors, outcome adjudicators, data analysts, and data safety and monitoring committee).*

Setting: 45 hospitals in Japan.

Patients: 203 patients aged ≥ 18 years (mean age 76 y; 56% men; median National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score 22) who had acute ischemic stroke, occlusion of the internal carotid or M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery, NIHSS score ≥ 6, Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomographic Score (ASPECTS) value of 3 to 5, and modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0 or 1 before stroke onset; were randomized

within 6 hours of time last known well or 6 to 24 hours after last known well if there was no signal change in the initial image on fluid-attenuated inversion recovery; and for whom EVT could be initiated within 60 minutes of randomization. Key exclusions: clinically meaningful cerebral mass effect with midline shift, acute intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), or estimated high risk for hemorrhage.

Interventions: EVT plus medical care ( n = 101) or medical care alone ( n = 102). EVT could include stent retriever, aspiration catheter, balloon angioplasty, intracranial stent, and carotid artery stent and was selected by physicians. Medical care was consistent with guidelines from the American Heart Association and American Stroke Association.

Funding: Mihara Cerebrovascular Disorder Research Promotion Fund and Japanese Society for Neuroendovascular Therapy.

*See Glossary. Some information provided by authors.

Commentary: Previous EVT trials underrepresent Asian populations, leaving uncertainty about the feasibility of mechanical thrombectomy in such patients with intracranial atherosclerosis -related LVO (1).

The trial by Yoshimura and colleagues showed that, in previously well-functioning patients with acute stroke and ASPECTS values of 3 to 5 in Japan, EVT improved outcomes vs. usual medical care alone. Included patients had large ischemic regions (less salvageable brain tissue and lower likelihood of a good outcome), whereas most previous trials excluded or had limited numbers of these patients. Although most EVT trials define a good outcome as an mRS score of 0 to 2 (2), patients with scores of 3 are still likely to have functional independence with similar quality of life as patients with scores of 2 (3).

Rates of post-EVT ICH increase in patients with larger regions of ischemic tissue (2). Although ICH occurred more frequently after EVT, groups did not differ for symptomatic ICH. Because EVT improved 90-day functional outcomes (number needed to treat = 6), rate of ICH in this trial is probably not important from a patient perspective.

Although guidelines recommend EVT for patients with ASPECTS ≥ 6 (4), this trial suggests more favorable outcomes with EVT in patients with a lower ASPECTS; EVT should be offered to patients who otherwise would not qualify for EVT with an internal carotid or M1 segment acute ischemic stroke.

Bottom line:

In patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by large-vessel occlusion and a large ischemic region, adding EVT to medical care increased the likelihood of better function at 90 days.
