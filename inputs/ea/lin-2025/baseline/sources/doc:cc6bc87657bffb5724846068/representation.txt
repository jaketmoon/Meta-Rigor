In large acute ischemic stroke, adding endovascular thrombectomy to medical therapy improved function at 90 d

Sarraj A, Hassan AE, Abraham MG, et al; SELECT2 Investigators. Trial of endovascular thrombectomy for large ischemic strokes. N Engl J Med. 2023;388:1259-1271.

Question: In patients with acute ischemic stroke with a large ischemic-core volume, what is the effect of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) within 24 hours of stroke onset added to standard medical therapy vs. standard medical therapy alone on functional outcomes?

Design: Randomized controlled trial (SELECT2 [Randomized Controlled Trial to Optimize Patient's Selection for Endovascular Treatment in Acute Ischemic Stroke]). Trial was stopped early for efficacy.

Blinding: Treatment allocation concealed; blinded (data collectors and 90-day outcome assessors).*

Setting: 31 sites in Australia, Canada, Europe, New Zealand, and USA.

Patients: 352 patients aged 18 to 85 years (median age, 67 y; 59% men; median Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score

[ASPECTS], 4) who had acute ischemic stroke due to occlusion of the internal carotid artery (cervical or intracranial) or the M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery or both, with a large ischemic core on noncontrast computed tomography (CT) (ASPECTS value = 3 to 5; score range, 0 to 10; lower value = larger infarction) or estimated ischemic-core volume ≥ 50 mL on CT perfusion imaging, prestroke modified Rankin scale (mRs) score of 0 to 1 (no disability), and no evidence of intracranial hemorrhage.

Interventions: EVT plus standard medical therapy according to local protocols and international guidelines ( n = 178) or standard medical therapy alone ( n = 174).

Funding:

Stryker Neurovascular.

*See Glossary. Some information provided by authors.

Commentary: EVT is currently recommended for acute ischemic stroke in patients presenting ≤ 24 hours from symptom onset with large-vessel occlusion and small ischemic core volume (ASPECTS ≥ 6 and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale disability score ≥ 6) (1). The SELECT2 trial evaluated whether EVT in patients with large ischemic-core volume leads to better functional outcomes than standard medical therapy alone, including IV thrombolytics for those who were eligible.

The SELECT2 trial included only patients with excellent baseline function (i.e., mRs of 0 or 1). Overall, 20% of patients in the EVT group achieved functional independence at 90 days compared with 7% in the medical therapy group. Of note, 18.5% of patients in the EVT group had procedural complications, including arterial access-site complications (hematoma and infection), vascular dissection, arterial perforation, and intraprocedural vasospasm.

The focus on pragmatic outcomes, including functional independence, procedural complications, and quality of life, is a strength of the trial. A major limitation is that it was stopped early, which introduces bias and possibly overestimates the treatment effect.

A growing body of evidence shows improved function with EVT for acute ischemic stroke with large ischemic burden (2). The SELECT2 trial supports considering

Bottom line:

In patients with acute ischemic stroke with a large ischemic core volume, adding EVT to standard medical therapy improved functional outcomes at 90 days vs. standard medical therapy alone.
