THERAPEUTICS

In acute ischemic stroke with a large core infarct, endovascular thrombectomy reduced disability at 90 d

Costalat V, Jovin TG, Albucher JF, et al; LASTE Trial Investigators. Trial of thrombectomy for stroke with a large infarct of unrestricted size. N Engl J Med. 2024;390:1677-1689.

Question: In patients who present within 6.5 hours of acute ischemic stroke due to occlusion of a proximal artery in the anterior circulation with a large baseline infarct, what are the efficacy and safety of adding endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) to medical care?

Design: Randomized controlled trial (LASTE [Large Stroke Therapy Evaluation] trial). Recruitment was stopped early after a published trial showed benefit of EVT in similar patients.

Blinding: Treatment allocation concealed; blinded (adjudicators of imaging and angiography data).*

Setting: 30 stroke centers in France and Spain.

Patients: 333 patients aged >18 years (median age, 74 y; 52% men; median National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score, 21) who had Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score (ASPECTS) values ≤ 5 on computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (ASPECTS

values of 4 or 5 in patients aged >80 y), intracranial occlusion of the internal carotid artery and/or occlusion of the proximal segment of the middle cerebral artery, prestroke modified Rankin scale (mRS) scores of 0 or 1, and NIHSS scores ≥ 6; and could be randomly assigned within 6.5 hours of known onset of symptoms. Patients with unknown time of symptom onset but within 24 hours after they were last known to be well could be included if the lesion on diffusion-weighted MRI had no corresponding lesion on fluid-attenuation inversion recovery MRI. Key exclusion: intracerebral hemorrhage.

Interventions: EVT plus medical care ( n = 166) or medical care alone ( n = 167).

Funding: Montpellier University Hospital through an unrestricted grant from an industry consortium of medical device companies (Medtronic, Stryker, Balt Extrusion, MicroVention, and Cerenovus).

*See Glossary.

Commentary: EVT is superior to best medical management for reducing disability in patients with acute ischemic stroke from a large artery occlusion (1), but its safety and benefit in patients with the largest ischemic cores with ASPECT values ≤ 2 has remained unclear (2).

The LASTE trial from Costalat and colleagues showed that, in patients with large infarcts of unrestricted size, adding early EVT to usual care improved functional outcomes and reduced mortality. These results are consistent with recent trials of patients with large core infarcts at baseline, although LASTE is 1 of only 2 trials to include patients with ASPECT values <2, with 56% of the included population having values ≤ 2 (2).

Recent large-core studies -especially LASTE, which included the largest cores -represent a shift in acute stroke management but there are some limitations. LASTE enrolled patients with symptom onset <6.5 hours and only included patients with the lowest ASPECTS values (0 to 3) if they were aged <80 years. Most patients were enrolled based on MRI, which is not readily available across the world. The median 90-day mRS score was 4 (moderately severe disability, unable to walk or attend to bodily needs without assistance), but the percentage of moderate and severe disability did not increase with reduction in mortality. Importantly, 10% of patients treated with EVT had symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, which is higher than in trials of smaller infarct cores (1).

Although the results of the LASTE trial may not be generalizable to all patients, they highlight that patient selection is crucial for good outcomes. Patient's wishes and these results, with their limitations, should be considered when offering EVT to patients.

Bottom line:

In patients who present within 6.5 hours of acute ischemic stroke due to occlusion of a proximal artery in the anterior circulation with a large baseline infarct, adding EVT to medical care reduced disability and death at 90 days.
