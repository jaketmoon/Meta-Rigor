IMPORTANCE Endovascular therapy (EVT) demonstrated better outcomes compared with medical management in recent randomized clinical trials (RCTs) of patients with large infarct.

OBJECTIVE To compare outcomes of EVT vs medical management across different strata of the Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score (ASPECTS) and infarct core volume in patients with large infarct.

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS This prespecified secondary analysis of subgroups of the Endovascular Therapy in Acute Anterior Circulation Large Vessel Occlusive Patients With a Large Infarct Core (ANGEL-ASPECT) RCT included patients from 46 stroke centers across China between October 2, 2020, and May 18, 2022. Participants were enrolled within 24 hours of symptom onset and had ASPECTS of 3 to 5 or 0 to 2 and infarct core volume of 70 to 100 mL. Patients were divided into 3 groups: ASPECTS of 3 to 5 with infarct core volume less than 70 mL, ASPECTS of 3 to 5 with infarct core volume of 70 mL or greater, and ASPECTS of 0 to 2.

INTERVENTIONS Endovascular therapy or medical management.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES The primary outcome was the ordinal 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score.

RESULTS There were 455 patients in the trial; median age was 68 years (IQR, 60-73 years), and 279 (61.3%) were male. The treatment effect did not vary significantly across the 3 baseline imaging subgroups ( P = .95 for interaction). The generalized odds ratio for the shift in the 90-day mRS distribution toward better outcomes with EVT vs medical management was 1.40 (95% CI, 1.06-1.85; P = .01) in patients with ASPECTS of 3 to 5 and infarct core volume less than 70 mL, 1.22 (95% CI, 0.81-1.83; P = .23) in patients with ASPECTS of 3 to 5 and infarct core volume of 70 mL or greater, and 1.59 (95% CI, 0.89-2.86; P = .09) in patients with ASPECTS of 0 to 2.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE In this study, no significant interaction was found between baseline imaging status and the benefit of EVT compared with medical management in patients with large infarct core volume. However, estimates within subgroups were underpowered. A pooled analysis of large core trials stratified by ASPECTS and infarct core volume strata is warranted.

Original Investigation Research

T he benefit of endovascular therapy (EVT) for patients with acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO) with large infarct has been proven by 4 randomizedclinical trials (RCTs). 1-6 Themechanicalthrombectomyfor large brain infarctions (MAGNA) individual patient-level data meta-analysis of 3 RCTs (Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-Acute Embolism-Japan Large Ischemic Core Trial [RESCUE-JapanLIMIT], 5 RandomizedControlledTrialto Optimize Patient's Selection for Endovascular Treatment in AcuteIschemicStroke[SELECT2], 4 andEndovascularTherapy in Acute Anterior Circulation Large Vessel Occlusive Patients With a Large Infarct Core [ANGEL-ASPECT] 3 ) presented at the European Stroke Conference (ESOC) 2023 7 and 2 other study-level meta-analyses including RCTs and observational studies 8,9 also confirmed the efficacy of EVT for such patients. However, the Thrombectomy for Emergent Salvage of Large Anterior Circulation Ischemic Stroke (TESLA) RCT presented at ESOC 2023 did not demonstrate that EVT was superior to medical management for such patients according to the utility-weighted modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at 90 days, falling just short of the prespecified primary outcome threshold to confirm efficacy. 7

In general, a large infarct is defined by an Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography (CT) Score (ASPECTS) less than 6 on a scale from 0 to 10 (with lower scores indicating a larger territory of ischemic change) or an infarct core volumegreater than 50 mL or 70 mL. 10-12 However, considering the variability of ASPECTS interpretation of non-contrastenhanced CT (NCCT) and the problem of overestimating infarct core volume by CT perfusion in the early window, large infarct assessed by only 1 imaging modality may result in inaccurate selection of patients with large infarct who may be candidates for EVT. 13-15 Therefore, the ANGEL-ASPECT RCT 3 combined baseline ASPECTS and infarct core volume to assess large infarct. We hypothesized that a mismatch between ASPECTSandinfarctcorevolumemayexist,whichcouldhelp select patients with large infarct who are most likely to benefit from EVT. Thus, the current study sought to compare the outcomes of EVT with those of medical management in patients with (1) baseline ASPECTS of 3 to 5 and infarct core volume less than 70 mL, (2) baseline ASPECTS of 3 to 5 and infarct core volumeof70mLorgreater,and(3)baselineASPECTS of 0 to 2 based on data from the ANGEL-ASPECT RCT. 3

We aimed to further investigate whether EVT would be superior to medical management with respect to functional recovery among patients with a large infarct core due to acute anterior circulation LVO. Study Outcomes

Theprimaryoutcomeofthisanalysiswasthe90-daymRSordinal distribution. Statistical Analysis

Wedescribedcontinuousandordinalvariablesasmedian(IQR) and categorical variables as number (percentage). The Pearson χ 2 test or Fisher exact test for categorical variables and Kruskal-Wallis test for continuous variables were conducted to compare baseline characteristics among the 3 groups. Alackofassociation between infarct core volume growth and patient clinical outcomes within the 3 imaging subgroups of our analysis may suggest the limitation of using infarct volume growth as a surrogate imaging marker for outcomes when studying the treatment effect of cerebroprotection therapies 24 in patients with large ischemic infarct. Individual patient-level pooling of data across the RCTs of large ischemic infarct may increase sample size power to study whetheracorrelationbetweeninfarctvolumegrowthandclinical outcomes exists in patients with large ischemic infarct.

With regard to safety outcomes, another finding in our study was that EVT was associated with an increased risk of ICH within 48 hours in both the group with baseline ASPECTSof3to5andinfarctcorevolumelessthan70mLand the group with baseline ASPECTS of 3 to 5 and infarct core volumeof70mLorgreaterbutnotinthegroupwithbaseline ASPECTSof0to2.However,weobservedthat EVT was associated with increased risk of ICH in patients with LVO with ASPECTS of 0 to 2 compared with medical management.

Limitations

Thepresentstudyhasseverallimitationswheninterpretingthe findings. First, the reduced sample size as partitioned betweenthe3subgroupsmayhavelimitedourability to detect a difference in treatment effect by baseline imaging status. The samplesizesofthegroupwithASPECTSof3to5andinfarctcore volume of 70 mL or greater and the group with ASPECTS of 0 to 2 were not large enough to confirm the safety or efficacy of EVT. However, we observed a trend for better outcomes in the EVT group than in the medical management group in both of those subgroups. Further pooled analysis of the RCTs is needed to investigate this topic to improve precision estimates within these subgroups with large infarct core volume. Second,somevariables,suchassocioeconomicconditions,sex differences, 25 complex ethnic differences, and postdischarge factors, could not be measured, which may confoundourfindings. Third, the subgroup analysis was underpowered, which mighthaveledtoatypeIIerrorduetotherelativelysmallsample size. Fourth,whileourplantostudysubgroupsbyASPECTSand infarct core volume was prespecified in the trial protocol, we did not specify the primary and secondary outcomes of this analysis. Thus,ourfindingsarehypothesisgenerating.Fifth,as the participants in the ANGEL-ASPECT trial 3 were all Chinese, our findings may not be generalizable to other populations.

Conclusions

Ourstudyofpatientswithlargeischemiccorevolumefoundthat amongthe3imagingsubgroups,thebenefitofEVTdidnotvary byASPECTSorinfarctcorevolumestrata.However,whenstudyingeachsubgroup,EVTwasassociatedwithbetterclinicaloutcomesthanmedicalmanagementbutwithanincreasedriskof anyICHinpatientswithASPECTSof3to5andaninfarctcorevolume less than 70 mL. The findings suggest that patients with ASPECTSof3to5andaninfarctcorevolumeof70mLorgreater maybenefit from EVT with regard to independent ambulation butwithanincreasedriskofanyICH.Theoutcomeswerenotdifferent betweenEVTandmedicalmanagementinpatientswith baselineASPECTSof0to2.Furtherstudieswithalargersample size and involving other ethnic populations are warranted.
