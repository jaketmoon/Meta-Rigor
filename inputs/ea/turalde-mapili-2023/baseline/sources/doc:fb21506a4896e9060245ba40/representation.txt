2. Section S2. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion Criteria

Participants met all of the following criteria:

For the preterm cohort (excluding infants with hemodynamically significant congenital heart disease [CHD] or chronic lung disease of immaturity [CLD]): preterm infants in their first year of life and born ≤35 weeks 0 days gestational age eligible to receive palivizumab in accordance with national or local guidelines, including those with:

(a) Uncomplicated small atrial or ventricular septal defects or patent ductus arteriosus, or

7

[DOCLING_PAGE 9]

(b) Aortic stenosis, pulmonic stenosis, or coarctation of the aorta alone.

2. For the CHD/CLD cohort:

(a) Infants with CHD - infants in their first year of life and documented, hemodynamically significant CHD (must be unoperated or partially corrected CHD). Note: Infants with hemodynamically significant acyanotic cardiac lesions must have pulmonary hypertension (≥40 mmHg measured pressure in the pulmonary artery) or the need for daily medication to manage CHD

(b) Infants with CLD - infants in their first year of life and a diagnosis of CLD of prematurity requiring medical intervention/management (i.e., supplemental oxygen, bronchodilators, or diuretics) within the 6 months prior to randomization.

Infants who are entering their first respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) season at the time of screening.

Written informed consent and any locally required authorization (e.g., Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act in the USA, EU Data Privacy Directive in the EU) obtained from the infant's parent(s)/legal representative(s) prior to performing any protocol-related procedures, including screening evaluations.

Infant's parent(s)/legal representative(s) able to understand and comply with the requirements of the protocol including follow-up and illness visits as judged by the investigator.

Infant is available to complete the follow-up period, which will be 1 year after Season 1/ Dose 1 for infants without CHD/CLD, or 1 year after Season 2/Dose 1 (or last replacement dose as applicable for CHD) for infants with CHD/CLD.

Exclusion Criteria

Any of the following excluded the participants from participation in the trial:

Any fever (≥100.4°F [≥38.0°C], regardless of route) or acute illness within 7 days prior to randomization.

Any history of lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) or active LRTI prior to, or at the time of, randomization.

Known history of RSV infection or active RSV infection prior to, or at the time of, randomization.

Hospitalization at the time of randomization, unless discharge is expected within the 7 days after randomization.

Requirement for mechanical ventilation, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, continuous positive airway pressure, or other mechanical respiratory or cardiac support at the time of randomization.

Anticipated cardiac surgery within 2 weeks after randomization.

Anticipated survival of <6 months after randomization.

Any known allergy, including to immunoglobulin products, or history of allergic reaction.

Receipt of palivizumab or other RSV monoclonal antibody or any RSV vaccine, including maternal RSV vaccination.

Receipt of any monoclonal or polyclonal antibody (for example, hepatitis B immunoglobulin, intravenous immunoglobulin) or anticipated use during the study.

Any condition that, in the opinion of the investigator, would interfere with evaluation of the study drug or interpretation of participant safety or study results.

Concurrent enrollment in another interventional study.

3. Section S3. Trial Design and Oversight

This was a pivotal Phase 2/3, randomized, double-blind, palivizumab-controlled trial to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics (PK), anti-drug antibody (ADA) response, and descriptive efficacy of nirsevimab in infants with CHD, CLD or born preterm and eligible to receive palivizumab when entering their first or second RSV season (Season 1 or Season 2, respectively). It was planned to enroll approximately 900 palivizumab-eligible infants entering their first RSV season into one of 2 cohorts: (1) preterm cohort, including approximately 60 0 preterm infants (≤35 weeks gestational age) without C HD or CLD, or (2) CHD/CLD cohort, including approximately 300 infants with hemodynamically significant CHD or CLD of prematurity. A minimum of 100 infants with hemodynamically significant CHD were to be enrolled. Within each cohort, randomization was to be stratified by hemisphere (northern, southern) and participant age at the time of Season 1 randomization (≤3 .0 months, >3.0 to ≤6 .0 months, and >6.0 months).

Participants were randomized 2:1 to receive a single fixed intra-muscular (IM) dose of nirsevimab followed by 4 once-monthly IM doses of placebo one IM injection of nirsevimab 50 mg (if <5 kg) or 100 mg (if ≥5 kg) or placebo. The nirsevimab dose level was stratified by weight band: i.e., 50 mg for infants <5 kg or 100 mg for infants ≥5 kg. Participants in the palivizumab group received 5 once-monthly IM doses of 15 mg/kg palivizumab.

Infants in the CHD/CLD cohort who underwent cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass after receipt of Dose 1, but prior to receipt of Dose 5, were to receive a replacement dose of the study drug they received for Dose 1 immediately following the surgery, once they were determined to be medically stable for an IM injection. Subsequent doses were given according to the protocol-specified dosing schedule.

10

[DOCLING_PAGE 12]

Infants in the preterm cohort were followed through 1 year after Season 1/Dose 1, and infants in the CHD/CLD cohort were to be followed through 1 year after Season 2/Dose 1. Infants in the CHD/CLD cohort who received a replacement dose in Season 2 were to be followed through 1 year after the last replacement dose.

Infants in the CHD/CLD cohort <7 kg at the time of dosing for Season 2 were to have increased monitoring of adverse events (AEs). Any serious AEs or Grade 3 or Grade 4 AEs that occurred in the first month after dosing were to be sent to the independent data monitoring committee for immediate review.

Infants were monitored throughout the study for LRTI. All patients seeking medical attention for a respiratory illness (in either the inpatient or outpatient setting) were evaluated for LRTI, including protocol-defined medically attended RSV LRTI. All infants evaluated for LRTI had respiratory samples obtained and tested centrally for RSV using the United States Food and Drug Administration-cleared and Conformité Européenne-marked in vitro diagnostic real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) assay.

Blood samples were collected for PK and ADA at Screening or Day 1 predose and on Days 31 (predose), 151, and 361 (for the CHD/CLD cohort, prior to Season 2 dosing); Season 2 for the CHD/CLD cohort only - Days 31 (predose), 151, and 361. In Japan, an additional blood sample was collected on Day 8. In Europe, blood sample for pharmacokinetics analysis was collected on Day 15 instead of Day 31. Additionally, samples were collected during both seasons from all infants hospitalized for a respiratory infection, and before and after cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass for infants with CHD requiring a replacement dose of study drug. Evaluation was performed using validated immunoassays. The PK of nirsevimab were to be determined as described previously. 1 Anti-nirsevimab antibodies and anti-palivizumab antibodies were assessed for nirsevimab and palivizumab groups, respectively ; a positive antibody response was defined as a titer of ≥50 for anti-nirsevimab antibodies, and a titer of ≥30 for anti -palivizumab antibodies. 1,2 Tiered analyses were performed to include screening, confirmatory, and titer assay components, and the positive-negative cut points were statistically determined from drug-naive validation samples.

The trial was performed in accordance with the provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Conference on Harmonisation. Each site had approval from an institutional ethics review board or ethics committee and written informed consent was obtained for each participant. 4. Section S4. Setting and Locations

Participants were enrolled prior to the RSV season in 2019 and 2020 across 126 sites from 25 countries (Northern Hemisphere: Austria [1 site], Belgium [2 sites], Bulgaria [10 sites], Canada [2 sites], Czech Republic [1 site], Estonia [2 sites], Finland [1 site], France [9 sites], Germany [3 sites], Hungary [5 sites], Italy [2 sites], Japan [6 sites], Latvia [3 sites], Lithuania [2 sites], Mexico [2 sites], Poland [5 sites], Republic of Korea [3 sites], Russia [7 sites], Spain [11 sites], Sweden [1 site], Turkey [3 sites], Ukraine [8 sites], United Kingdom [3 sites], and United States [27 sites]; Southern Hemisphere: South Africa [7 sites]).

5. Section S5. Randomization and Blinding

Once informed consent was obtained, a subject identification (SID) number was assigned by an interactive web response system. The SID number was used to identify the participant during the screening process and throughout study participation. A master log of all consented participants was maintained at each site and documented all screening failures including the reason(s) for screening failure.

The trial was blinded, and all authors had access to the aggregated analysis. Neither the participant/legal representative nor the investigator or any of the site staff who were involved in the treatment or clinical evaluation of the participants were aware of the treatment received. In the event that treatment allocation for a participant became known to the investigator or other blinded study staff involved in the management of study participants, the sponsor was to be notified immediately. Sites used commercially available saline as the placebo; syringe barrels were covered up to maintain the study blind.

6. Section S6. Endpoints

The primary endpoint of the study was the safety and tolerability of nirsevimab. Secondary endpoints included evaluation of serum concentrations and PK parameters of nirsevimab and palivizumab; incidence of ADAs to nirsevimab and palivizumab; and incidence of medically attended RSV LRTI and hospitalizations due to confirmed RSV through 150 days after Dose 1 for Season 1 and Season 2. All RSV cases were confirmed by central laboratory testing (Lyra RSV + hMPV, Quidel; San Diego, CA, USA).

AEs were graded by severity according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events and coded by Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities. Hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis), immune complex disease, and thrombocytopenia were designated as AEs of special interest.

7. Section S7. Statistical Methods

Sample Size

With respect to safety, 600 infants exposed to nirsevimab during Season 1 would provide a 95% probability of observing at least 1 AE if the true event rate was 0.5%; if no AEs were observed, this study would provide 95% confidence that the true event rate was <0.5%.

Statistical Analyses

There are three planned analyses for this study: the primary analysis; Season 2 analysis; and the final analysis. The primary analysis was conducted after all randomized participants had completed follow-up through the first 5-month RSV season (i.e., Season 1, Day 151 visit) and would include all available Season 1 safety, efficacy, PK, and ADA data at the time of the data cutoff. Details regarding the Season 2 analysis and final analysis were pre-specified in the SAP. All data presented in this letter were based on the primary analysis. The summary of primary cardiac lesions was generated post-hoc. All analyses were descriptive.

Safety

Safety of nirsevimab was summarized by treatment group based on the as-treated population (defined as all participants who receive any study drug analyzed according to treatment received). For participants who accidentally received both nirsevimab and palivizumab, those who received any amount of nirsevimab are included in the nirsevimab group for AE summaries; those who received nirsevimab as their first dose are included in the nirsevimab group for ADA summaries.

8. Section S8. Trial Limitations and Generalizability

This trial was conducted in infants who were eligible to receive palivizumab according to local standard of care guidelines, thereby ensuring the generalizability of the results to the population in whom palivizumab is currently used. In particular, this trial enrolled significant numbers of patients in vulnerable subpopulations, the preterm <29 weeks gestational age, hemodynamically significant CHD, and CLD.

While this trial enrolled and collected safety data during the COVID-19 pandemic, participants remained in contact with the investigator and, if an AE occurred, were assessed in person or by telemedicine. Of note, 531/925 (57.4%) infants were randomized after the start of the COVID-19 pandemic; 49/925 (5.3%) randomized infants (palivizumab: 15/309 [4.9%]; nirsevimab 34/616 [5.5%]) had important protocol deviations due to the COVID-19 pandemic (mostly comprising missed sequential doses of palivizumab or placebo after the initial dose).
