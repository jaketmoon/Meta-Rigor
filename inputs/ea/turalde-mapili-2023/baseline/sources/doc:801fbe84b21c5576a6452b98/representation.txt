Summary

Prevention of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection, which leads to lower respiratory tract illness in infants as well as in older people, has become a global public health priority. There is a need for an effective prophylactic RSV vaccine that can protect infants from RSV infection. The only approved agent for RSV prophylaxis is palivizumab, but its use is limited to high-risk infants. For one RSV season, 5 doses of palivizu­ mab are required, which increases the cost per season and does not enable optimal use of palivizumab in low- and middleincome countries. In this context, nirsevimab (MEDI8897), a monoclonal antibody derived from human IgG1, is being developed by MedImmune (AstraZeneca) and Sanofi, and tar -gets conformational epitope in pre-F specific antigenic site ɸ of RSV. Three amino acids (M257Y/S259T/T261E [YTE]) have been engineered for nirsevimab in the conserved Fc region, which extends the half-life of the antibody, so just 1 dose will be required for an entire RSV season. Nirsevimab shows a significant increase in potency against RSV compared to palivi -zumab. Phase I and II clinical studies found that nirsevimab is well tolerated and safe for clinical use, and a single dose prevented RSV infection for a whole season. Introduction

Globally, infants are at a high risk for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI). As estimated in 2015, on average, 33.1 million RSV LRTI episodes in children below 5 years of age, including 3.2 million hospitalizations and 118,000 deaths, are reported annually worldwide (1, 2). During the 2015-2016 season in the U.S., a multicenter surveillance found that among < 5-year-old children, RSV was associated with more than one-third of acute respiratory infections requiring hospitalization (3).
