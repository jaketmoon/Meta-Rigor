Supplementary Methods
TESLA Trial Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria: 1. 18 to 85 years of age
2. Presenting with symptoms consistent with an acute ischemic
stroke
3. Imaging evidence of an anterior circulation occlusion of the
Internal Carotid Artery (ICA) terminus and/or Middle Cerebral
Artery Main Stem (MCA M1) segment
4. NIHSS score ≥6 at the time of randomization
5. Ability to randomize within 24 hours of stroke onset
6. Pre-stroke mRS score 0-1
7. Ability to obtain signed informed consent
8. If indicated for use, IV rtPA should be administered as soon as
possible and no later than 3 hours from symptom onset

Imaging evidence of moderate-large infarct defined as:
1. NCCT ASPECTS 2-5
Exclusion Criteria: 1. Females who are pregnant, or those of child-bearing potential
with positive urine or serum beta Human Chorionic
Gonadotropin (HCG) test
2. Known severe allergy (more than a rash) to contrast media
uncontrolled by medications
3. Refractory hypertension (defined as persistent systolic blood
pressure >185 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure >110 mmHg)
4. CT evidence of the following conditions:
• Midline shift or herniation
• Evidence of intracranial hemorrhage
• Mass effect with effacement of the ventricles
5. Computed Tomography Angiography (CTA) evidence suggestive
of difficult endovascular access per the treating interventionalist
6. Presence of cervical ICA occlusion (e.g., related to
atherosclerotic disease or dissection)
7. Rapidly improving neurological status prior to randomization to
NIHSS <6
8. Bilateral strokes or multiple intracranial occlusions in multiple
territories
9. Intracranial tumors (except small meningiomas [≤3 cm])
10. Known hemorrhagic diathesis, coagulation factor deficiency, or
on anticoagulant therapy with an International Normalized Ratio
(INR) of >3.0 or Partial Thromboplastin Time (PTT) >3 times of
normal
11. Baseline platelet count <30,000 per microliter (µl)
12. Life expectancy less than 1 year prior to stroke onset
13. Participation in another randomized clinical trial that could
confound the evaluation of the study
14. Any other condition (in the opinion of the site investigator) that
precludes an endovascular procedure or poses a significant
hazard to the patient if an endovascular procedure was
performed

ASPECTS Training for Site Investigators
ASPECTS is a topographic map that divides the middle cerebral artery territory into 10 regions, with one point
deducted for each region that demonstrates acute ischemic changes on noncontrast CT.1 ASPECTS was determined
by local site investigators who were trained and certified in ASPECTS scoring, either by passing an online testing
module consisting of 10 training cases and 20 test cases 2 or a case-based review with the principal investigators.
Approximately 85% (143/169) of the certified investigators successfully completed the online test.

Automated ASPECTS Software in TESLA
Installation of automated ASPECTS software (eASPECTS; Brainomix, Oxford, UK) was offered to the sites but not
required for trial participation. If the software was installed at a site, it was not allowed to be used for patient
selection into the trial; the software output was collected for preplanned secondary analyses.
A prespecified analysis of the primary outcome in the intention-to treat population with study-eligible ASPECTS as
determined by the automated software was not performed due to the lack of software availability at most sites which
precluded prospective processing of the software output and adjudication by the site PIs as described in the protocol.
Prospective adjudication by the site PIs was mandated because the automated software is not intended as a stand -
alone tool, but rather as a decision support tool which is meant to be reviewed and adjudicated by the physician to
determine the final score. Missing automated ASPECTS scores were generated retrospectively to perform the
analysis of ASPECTS agreement between site investigators, the core lab, and the automated software (Tables S7 to
S9 in the Supplementary Appendix), although the automated scores do not conform to the labeling for eASPECTS
as explained above.

Description of Consented but Not Randomized Patients
A total of 6 subjects were consented but not randomized. Two subjects (031-S02, 009-S01) had repeat noncontrast
CT after obtaining consent which showed an ASPECTS <2. One subject (031-S01) had a baseline mRS of 1 as
reported by an outside facility, but upon arrival, baseline mRS was reported to be 3 by family. One subject (049-
S01) was consented by a fellow during night shift who was unaware of the randomization process. This subject was
consented and immediately taken to thrombectomy. One subject (026-S01) is a case of mistaken identity. Another
patient with the same name and DOB presented with symptoms and was randomized after consent was obtained
from the LAR of 026-S01. This consented subject was not in the hospital and did not receive any treatment. One
subject (009-S02) was consented remotely at the outside hospital where they had tPA administered. The occlusion
recanalized by the time of arrival at the enrolling hospital. There were no major safety issues with these subjects.

eFigure 4. Forest Plot for Subgroup Analyses in the Primary Efficacy Analysis (As
Randomized) Population

The primary endpoint was the mean score for disability on the utility-weighted modified Rankin scale at 90 days. To determine the
utility-weighted score, the following weights are assigned to scores 0 through 6 on the modified Rankin scale: 10.0, 9.1, 7.6, 6.5, 3.3,
0, and 0, respectively. The utility-weighted modified Rankin scale ranges from 0 (death) to 10 (no symptoms or disability). Adjusted
differences in the primary endpoint were estimated with the use of a Bayesian general linear model with adjustment for baseline
ASPECTS. The subgroup thresholds were prespecified. The figure provides the number of patients randomized in each arm by
subgroup, the posterior mean differences and 95% credible intervals for intervention (thrombectomy) vs. control (medical
management alone) groups, and the probability that the treatment effect is positive within the subgroups. Each subgroup analysis
added a subgroup effect and an arm-subgroup interaction parameter to the primary analysis model. None of the subgroup analyses
demonstrated a significant interaction between the subgroup and the treatment benefit. A significant interaction was defined as a
Bayesian posterior probability of greater than 0.975 or less than 0.025, that the interaction parameter was positive. For perfusion
imaging analysis, core was defined as tissue with relative CBF <0.3, and penumbra as tissue with Tmax >6 seconds. Mismatch was
defined as a mismatch ratio >1.5, mismatch volume >50 ml, and core volume 70-120 ml for age ≤70 years or 70-100 ml for age >70
years. For first line thrombectomy approach, each subgroup in the intervention arm was compared to the entire control arm.

eFigure 6. Modified Rankin Scale Scores at 90 Days in the Primary Efficacy
Analysis Population with Study-Eligible Core Lab ASPECTS

The distribution of the modified Rankin scale scores is shown by treatment group. Scores range from 0 to 6, with 0 indicating no
symptoms, 1 no clinically significant disability, 2 slight disability (patient is able to look after own affairs without assistance but is
unable to carry out all previous activities), 3 moderate disability (patient requires some help but is able to walk unassisted), 4
moderately severe disability (patient is unable to attend to bodily needs without assistance and unable to walk unassisted), 5 severe
disability (patient requires constant nursing care and attention), and 6 death. The unadjusted common odds ratio for improvement
on the modified Rankin scale at 90 days with intervention was 1.53 (95% confidence interval, 0.96 to 2.45). In the intervention
group, one subject was lost to follow-up at 90 days; in the primary analysis, the 90-day mRS was imputed from the 30-day mRS for
this subject. In the control group, one subject was lost to follow-up at 90 days, and one subject withdrew consent at the day 5-7 visit;
in the primary analysis, the 90-day mRS was imputed from the 30-day mRS for the subject that was lost to follow-up.

eTables 16 and 17. Site Poolability Analysis in Primary Efficacy Analysis
Population
The results for the primary effcacy endpoint will be summarized by site and treatment arm using
descriptive statistics. The poolability of sites will be investigated by fitting a general linear model for the
weighted mRS outcome, with covariates for treatment arm, site, and the interaction of treatment arm and
site. A p-value less than 0.025 for the interaction term will be considered indication of a difference in site
treatment effects.
Sites with fewer than 10 subjects will be combined and treated as a single site when examining the
pooling. There were 44 sites, 34 of which had less than 10 subjects and were combined into a single site.
Thus, the general linear model used to test site poolability included 11 sites (one of which is composed of
34 small sites). Table S12 summarizes the observed weighted 90-day mRS by site across arms. Site 0
includes all sites that have fewer than 10 patients randomised. The F-test p-value for the test of the null
hypothesis that the treatment effect does not depend on site, is 0.00325, based on the F distribution with
10 and 275 degrees of freedom, indicating statistical evidence in favor of heterogeneity.
