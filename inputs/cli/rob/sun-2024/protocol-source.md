# Near-infrared spectroscopy-guided cerebral oxygenation management for prevention of postoperative delirium after cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol

## Review objective

To determine whether near-infrared spectroscopy monitoring with intervention for cerebral oxygen desaturation reduces postoperative delirium in adults undergoing cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass.

## Review question

In adults undergoing cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass, does near-infrared-spectroscopy-guided cerebral oxygenation management, compared with control care, reduce postoperative delirium and improve other eligible clinical outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Adults undergoing non-emergency cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass. Studies of pediatric surgery, emergency surgery, aortic dissection, or cardiac arrest will be excluded.

### Intervention

Near-infrared spectroscopy monitoring with protocolized intervention for cerebral oxygen desaturation.

### Comparator

Control care without an active near-infrared-spectroscopy-guided intervention.

### Study design

Randomized controlled trials reporting postoperative delirium. Reviews, case reports, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, studies without original data, and studies with incomplete outcome data will be excluded.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, Embase, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure will be searched from database inception through 16 March 2024. Only English-language reports will be included.

## Study selection

Records will be imported into EndNote and deduplicated. Two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts and then assess full texts against the eligibility criteria. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion.

## Outcomes

### Primary outcome

- Incidence of postoperative delirium.

### Secondary outcomes

- Kidney-related adverse outcomes.
- Cardiac-related adverse outcomes.
- Postoperative intensive care unit length of stay.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract author, title, surgery type, participant numbers and ages in each group, intervention details, and eligible outcome data. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Two reviewers will independently assess risk of bias with the Cochrane tool across random-sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, and selective reporting.

## Review-method provenance

The published Review stated both that pairwise meta-analysis used a random-effects model and that fixed versus random effects would be selected from I². This internally inconsistent, outcome-dependent switching rule is retained only as provenance and does not govern the benchmark analysis.

## Data synthesis

Analyses will be conducted in Stata 16.0. Dichotomous outcomes will be summarized as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Intensive care unit length of stay will be summarized as a mean difference with a 95% confidence interval. All Target meta-analyses will use a random-effects model selected before inspecting Study effect estimates, Q, or I² because the eligible Review combines different cardiac operations, cerebral-oxygenation intervention protocols, delirium assessments, and trial-level adverse-outcome definitions.

Heterogeneity will be assessed with Cochran's Q, using P below 0.10 as significant, and I², with values above 50% considered substantial. Q and I² will describe observed heterogeneity but will not select or change the analysis model. When substantial heterogeneity is present, meta-regression and subgroup analysis will be used to investigate potential sources. If heterogeneity remains unexplained, results will be synthesized descriptively.

## Reporting-bias assessment

Publication bias will be examined with Egger's test, Begg's test, and a trim-and-fill procedure.
