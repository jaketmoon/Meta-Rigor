Supplementary Methods:

Data management and site monitoring was overseen by Genentech, but the actual services of both were outsourced. The interim statistical reporting was done by an independent data coordinating center, which followed the Genentech Statistical Analysis Plan. The independent data monitoring committee (iDMC) reviewed the data, and the sponsor remained blinded during the trial.

The sample-size calculation assumption was developed based on the results from the DEFUSE 3 and EXTEND-IA TNK trials. With this assumption, we estimated that 456 patients, after accounting for a 5% dropout rate, would provide at least 90% power to detect the difference of a shift towards improvement (ie, a lower score) in the 90-day modified Rankin Scale between the two treatment arms at a two-sided 0.049 significance level, after adjusting for one interim efficacy analysis. Type I error levels were determined according to the Lan-DeMets theory for alpha-spending function that approximates the O'Brien-Fleming boundary.

Two safety-only interim analyses were planned and conducted when the first 25 and 50 patients had completed the 72-to-96-hours visit assessment after randomization. Additionally, per request from the independent data monitoring committee, two safety data reviews were later added that were planned to occur when the first 100 and 200 patients had completed the 72-to-96-hours visit assessment after randomization. Nonbinding sequential boundaries were used to monitor the symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) rate at those interim safety reviews in order to halt the trial if an excessive number of sICH cases was observed.

Moreover, efficacy analyses were adjusted for baseline factors, which included the randomization stratification factors (i.e., baseline age group, vessel occlusion location, baseline NIHSS, and endovascular capability at the enrolling site), protocol version (version 1-4 vs 5), and the intention to perform EVT at the time of randomization.

15

[DOCLING_PAGE 16]

The final set of adjustment variables varied by the outcome being analyzed and was determined by a pre-specified algorithm that ensured a minimum sample size was met in all cells formed by the combination of the adjustments. Missing data in the primary outcome were imputed using a last observation carry forward (LOCF) approach of the most recent post study baseline. Patients who had no post-baseline mRS value were not imputed and excluded from the analysis.

In Table 2, analyses were adjusted for protocol version (14 vs 5) and age (≤70 vs >70 years) for all outcomes. In addition, modified Rankin Scale at Day 90, functional independence at Day 90, recanalization at 24 hours, reperfusion at 24 hours, and good recovery in Glasgow Outcome Score at Day 90 were adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2), and reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT was adjusted for baseline NIHSS score (≤15 vs >15).

In Figure 2, age subgroups were adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and baseline NIHSS (≤15 vs >15); the subgroup with age <80 years was additionally adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2). NIHSS subgroups were adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and ag e (≤70 vs >70 years); the NIHSS ≤15 subgroup was additionally adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2). Subgroups for time between stroke onset to randomization were adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2); additionally, the subgroup that had <16 hours between stroke onset to randomization was adjusted for age (≤70 vs >70 years). Subgroups for planned EVT (planned or not planned) were adjusted for protocol version (14 vs 5); additionally, the subgroup with planned EVT was also adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2) and age (≤70 vs >70 years). Both subgroups for performed EVT (performed or not performed) were adjusted for protocol version (14 vs 5); additionally, the subgroup that underwent EVT was also adjusted for age (≤70 vs >70 years) and baseline NIHSS (≤15 vs >15). For the subgroup for which EVT was not performed, the model was also adjusted for EVT planned (yes vs no). All three subgroups for elapsed time between trial drug administration and groin puncture were adjusted for protocol version (14 vs 5); additionally, the ≤6 minutes subgroup was also adjusted for baseline NIHSS (≤15 vs >15), and the >6 to ≤22 minutes and >22 minutes subgroups were also adjusted for age (≤70 vs >70 years). All three subgroups for baseline infarct volume were adjusted for age (≤70 vs >70 years); additionally, the subgroups with baseline infarct volume <20 mL and ≥ 20 mL to <50 mL were also adjusted for protocol version (1 -4 vs 5). The subgroup with baseline infarct volume <20 mL was also adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2). Each occlusion subgroup was adjusted for age (≤70 vs >70 years); additionally, the M1 and M2 subgroups were also adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5), and the M1 subgroup was also adjusted for baseline NIHSS (≤15 vs >15).

Hypothesis testing of the secondary efficacy outcomes would be performed only if the primary efficacy analysis was statistically significant. To account for multiplicity, tests of the secondary outcomes were to be conducted sequentially, as detailed in the main text above and in study Statistical Analysis Plan, until a negative result indicated by a P value >0.049. P values were not provided for any subsequent analyses of the secondary outcomes following a negative test and the 95% CIs should not be used for hypothesis testing.

Multiple imputation was conducted as sensitivity analysis for the primary and several secondary outcomes (i.e., functional independence at 90 days, 24 hours recanalization, 24 hours reperfusion, and reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT), respectively.

For the primary outcome, mRS score at 90 days, missing data were imputed using a sequential regression method. Particularly, mRS was sampled from a multinomial distribution, that was estimated by ordinal logistic regressions, using baseline covariates and the previous mRS value. The ordinal logistic regressions estimated the cumulative odds for achieving an mRS outcome of at most 𝑘𝑘 :

The list of covariates included age, sex (male vs female), race (white vs. non-white), baseline NIHSS, occlusion type (ICA vs M1 vs M2) and discharge destination (home vs. non-home). Separate regressions were fitted for each treatment group to avoid any assumptions about the treatment effect.

The same imputation datasets were used for the analysis of functional independence at 90 days, which is just a dichotomization of the primary endpoint (i.e., mRS ≤ 2 vs mRS >2).

For 24 hours recanalization, 24 hours reperfusion, and reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT, missing data were imputed using a logistic regression model that included age, sex (male vs female), race (white vs. non-white), baseline NIHSS, occlusion type (ICA vs M1 vs M2), and EVT performed status (yes vs no) (except for the imputation of reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT, for which all patients included in the analysis received EVT) as covariates. The imputation model is specified as follows: 𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙 [Pr( 𝑌𝑌 = 1)] = 𝛼𝛼0 + 𝑋𝑋𝑋𝑋 , where 𝑌𝑌 represents the binary outcome variable (i.e., recanalization or reperfusion at 24 hours and reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT) with value 1 indicates the favorable outcome, 𝛼𝛼0 is the intercept, 𝑋𝑋 is the design matrix for the baseline covariates, and 𝛽𝛽 is the corresponding regression coefficient vector.

For all outcomes, 100 independent imputation replicates were generated, based on which statistical inferences were drawn using Rubin's Rules.
