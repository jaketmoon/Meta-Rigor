II. eMethods
eMethod 1. Patient Selection Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
1) Clinical diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke
2) Age >18 years
3)  Pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0-1
4) Disabling stroke defined as follows:
Baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score 6-25 at the time of randomization,
Or NIHSS 4-5 with disabling deficit (c.g. hemianopia, aphasia, loss of hand function) as determined by
the managing clinician
5) Symptom onset within 4.5-24h of treatment initiation (onset time refers to “last-seen-well”)
6) Written informed consent from patients or legally responsible representatives
Imaging inclusion criteria
7) Target mismatch profile on computed tomography perfusion (CTP) defined as an ischemic core
volume (using regional cerebral blood flow (tCBF)<30% on CTP) <50mL, a mismatch ratio (time-
to-maximum (Tmax)>6 sec volume to ischemic core ratio) >1.2, and a mismatch volume (Tmax>6
sec volume- ischemic core volume) >10mL
Exclusion Criteria
1) Treatment with a thrombolytic within the last 72 hours or intention to receive intravenous
thrombolysis
2) Contraindication to thrombolysis
3) Planned or anticipated treatment with endovascular therapy
4) Rapidly improving symptoms, particularly if in the judgement of the managing clinician is likely to
result in a NIHSS <4 at randomization
5) Pregnancy or lactating; formal testing needed in women of childbearing potential
6) Cerebral neoplasm (with mass effect)
7) Hereditary or acquired hemorrhagic diathesis, coagulation factor deficiency
8) Impairment in coagulation due to comorbid disease or anticoagulant use. If on warfarin, international
normalized ratio (INR) >1.7 or prothrombin time >15s; if use of any direct oral anticoagulant within
the last 48 hours; if use of heparin/heparinoid within the last 24 hours
9) Use of glycoprotein IIb-IIla inhibitors within the last 72 hours
10) Baseline platelet count <100,000/1L.
11) Undergoing hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis; known severe renal insufficiency with glomerular
filtration rate <30mL/min or serum creatinine >220mmol/L (2.5mg/dL)
12) Suspected aortic dissection
13) Major surgery or biopsy within the last 1 month
14) Any active bleeding or recent bleeding within the last 1 month, e.g. gastrointestinal or urinary tract
hemorrhage
15) Known severe, life-threatening allergy (more severe than skin rash) to contrast agents
16) Severe, uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure >185mmHg or diastolic blood pressure
>110mmHg)
17) Any terminal illness such that the patient would not be expected to survive more than half a year
18) Current participation in any investigational study that may confound outcome assessment of the
study
19) Any condition that, in the judgement of the investigator, is inappropriate for participation in the trial
or could impose hazards to the patient (e.g. inability to understand and/or follow the study
procedures and/or follow-up due to mental disorders, cognitive or emotional disorders
Imaging exclusion criteria
20) Acute intracranial hemorrhage
21) Acute large vessel occlusion on magnetic resonance/ computed tomography angiography, including
internal carotid artery (ICA), middle cerebral artery M1 segment (MCA-M1), vertebral artery and
basilar artery

Middle cerebral artery (MCA) (Figure 1A):
MI segment: The M1 or sphenoidal segment runs from the artery origin horizontally to the limen
insulae. The M1 typically encompasses the entire MCA stem and initial short segments of the MCA
divisions, although sometimes the MCA stem can extend into M2.
M2 segment: At the insula, the MCA turns vertically as the M2 or insular segment, from which most
MCA cortical branches arise. The M2 branches will be further divided into three categories dominant,
co-dominant, or non-dominant. This classification system refers to the perfused tissue by the occluded
M2 branch. To determine the perfused tissue the rater will use whenever possible the tissue at risk in
ml derived from the perfusion tissue maps. If the tissue at risk is above 100 ml the M2 branch will be
deemed dominant, if it is between 90 and 100 ml it will be deemed co-dominant and if it is below 90
ml it will be deemed non-dominant?
MB segment: The M3 or opercular segment then courses inferolaterally to the lateral surface of

M4 segment: The M4 or cortical segment begins as vessels exit the Sylvian fissure and fanover the

Al segment: The Al segment extends from the artery’s origin from the internal carotid artery to the

P1 segment: The P1 segment is located within the interpeduncular cistern and courses from the

P2 segment: The P2 segment arises from the posterior communicating artery, curves around the

P3 segment: The P3 segment runs through the quadrigeminal cistern and gives off anterior and

PICA: PICA originates near the ventral portion of the olivary body, ascends to the inferior border of
the pons, then descends along the lateral border of the fourth ventricle, reaching the undersurface of the
cerebellum and dividing into two branches, the medial and lateral branches.
AICA: AICA originates off the caudal third of the basilar artery and supplies the ventral and inferior
surface of the cerebellum and the lower lateral pons.

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage defined according to the Heidelberg bleeding

classification

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage: new intracranial hemorrhage detected by brain imaging

associated with any of the item below:

1. >4 points total NIHSS at the time of diagnosis compared to immediately before worsening. Note
that a 4 points change is not compared with the baseline admission NIHSS score but instead to the
immediate pre-deterioration neurological status.

2. 2 points in one NIHSS category. The rationale for this is to capture new hemorrhages that
produce new neurological symptoms, making them clearly symptomatic but not causing
worsening in the original stroke territory. For example, a new remote hemorrhage in the
contralateral occipital lobe may cause new hemianopia that is clearly symptomatic but the patient
will not have worsening of >4 points on the NIHSS score.

3. Leading to intubation/hemicraniectomy/EVD placement or other major medical/surgical
intervention.

4. — Absence of alternative explanation for deterioration.

Establishing the relatedness of deterioration and imaging findings: In patients with neurological

deterioration the relationship between clinical deterioration as defined and the hemorrhagic

event is:

Definite: if any intracranial hemorrhage is the dominant brain pathology on imaging causal for

deterioration.

Probable: in the presence of PH2, even if the ischemic infarct may have contributed to deterioration.

Possible: in the presence of HI2, PH1, remote from infarcted brain tissue, intraventricular hemorrhage,

Unlikely: in the presence of HII.

Definite and probable: hemorrhage shall be classified as symptomatic.

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage defined according to the SITS-MOST°

The definition of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage according to the SITS-MOST was local or
remote parenchymal hematoma type 2 on the imaging scan obtained 22 to 36 hours after treatment,
plus neurologic deterioration, as indicated by a score on the NIHSS that was higher by 4 points or more
than the baseline value or the lowest value between baseline and 24 hours, or hemorrhage leading to
death.

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage defined according to the ECASS II’

The definition according to the ECASS II was any hemorthage with neurologic deterioration, as
indicated by an NIHSS score that was higher by 4 points or more than the value at baseline or the
lowest value in the first 7 days, or any hemorrhage leading to death.

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage defined according to the ECASS III

The definition according to ECASS III was the same as that in ECASS II, plus the hemorrhage must
have been identified as the predominant cause of the neurologic deterioration.

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage defined according to the NINDS?

The definition according to the NINDS was any new hemorrhage associated with any neurologic
deterioration.

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage defined according to the IST-3"

The definition according to the IST-3 was significant neurological deterioration accompanied by clear
evidence of significant intracranial hemorthage on the post randomization scan (or autopsy if not
rescanned and death occurs after 7 days).

eMethod 3. Additional Statistical Section

Win Ratio

The win ratio is calculated by creating all possible pairs between the two groups and then dividing the
number of pairs in which a patient in the tenecteplase group exhibits a better clinical outcome by the
number of pairs in which a patient in the standard medical treatment group exhibits a better clinical
outcome.

Detailed description of the methodology can be found in the Wang and Pocock’s paper (2016)!".
The point estimate and 95% Cl of the unadjusted win ratio was estimated using the WINS package in
R. In addition, the adjusted win ratio for covariates was calculated using the IPTW method”.
Adjusted Analysis
We performed adjusted analysis of the primary outcome with adjustment for the following covariates:
age, baseline NIHSS score, circulation with steno-occlusion (anterior circulation and posterior
circulation, corresponding to the vessel occlusion location as defined in the protocol and statistical
analysis plan), and time from symptom onset to randomization.

To diagnose multicollinearity, variance inflation factors (VIF) were calculated within a linear
regression comprising all adjustment covariates. All obtained VIFs were below 2, suggesting negligible
multicollinearity!3,

A missing value in circulation with steno-occlusion was imputed from random values from a
uniform distribution with probabilities P; and P2 from the sample. The seed=224 was used.
Sensitivity Analysis
For the primary outcome, we performed sensitivity analysis accounting for the center effect. A
generalized estimating equation model with Poisson distribution and log link function was fitted to
estimate the risk ratio between the intervention and control treatment with treatment, and the center as a
cluster effect.

To remove the effect of rescue endovascular treatment, we performed a post hoc sensitivity
analysis in which the primary efficacy outcome for these patients was imputed based on baseline
characteristics using multiple imputation. The method of multiple imputation of missing primary
outcome was described in the statistical analysis plan.

Program Verification

Statistical programs for generating tables and figures were developed independently by both internal
and external statisticians before unblinding the trial using a faked treatment allocation in the same
dataset. The differences in statistical results were reconciled, and the consistent results were generated
and confirmed. After the trial unblinding, both internal and external statisticians ran their programs
using the true randomization allocation and generated and compared their statistical results for
consistency. A minor difference in decimal place was found in a secondary outcome and was corrected.
Final identical results were generated and agreed by both parties.
