Supplementary Methods:

          Data management and site monitoring was overseen by Genentech, but the

actual services of both were outsourced. The interim statistical reporting was done by an

independent data coordinating center, which followed the Genentech Statistical                                                              Analysis

Plan. The independent data monitoring committee (iDMC) reviewed the data, and the

sponsor remained blinded during the trial.

          The sample-size calculation assumption was developed based on the results

from the DEFUSE 3 and EXTEND-IA TNK trials. With this assumption, we estimated that

456 patients, after accounting for a 5% dropout rate, would provide at least 90% power

to detect the difference of a shift towards improvement (ie, a lower score) in the 90-day

modified Rankin Scale between the two treatment arms at a two-sided 0.049

significance level, after adjusting for one interim efficacy analysis. Type I error levels

were determined according to the Lan-DeMets theory for alpha-spending function that

approximates the O’Brien-Fleming boundary.

          Two safety-only interim analyses were planned and conducted when the first 25

and 50 patients had completed the 72-to-96-hours visit assessment after randomization.

Additionally, per request from the independent data monitoring committee, two safety

data reviews were later added that were planned to occur when the first 100 and 200

patients had completed the 72-to-96-hours visit assessment after randomization. Non-

binding sequential boundaries were used to monitor the symptomatic intracranial

hemorrhage (sICH) rate at those interim safety reviews in order to halt the trial                        if an

excessive number of sICH cases was observed.

          Moreover, efficacy analyses were adjusted for baseline factors , which included

the randomization stratification factors (i.e., baseline age group, vessel occlusion

location, baseline NIHSS, and endovascular capability at the enrolling site), protocol

version (version 1-4 vs 5), and the intention to perform EVT at the                                                                                                                                                                                               time of randomization.

                                                                                                                          15

The final set of adjustment variables varied by the outcome being analyzed and was

determined by a pre-specified algorithm that ensured a minimum sample size was met in

all cells formed by the combination of the adjustments. Missing data in the primary

outcome were imputed using a last observation carry forward (LOCF) approach of the

most recent post study baseline. Patients who had no post-baseline mRS value were not

imputed and excluded from the analysis.

         In Table 2, analyses were adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and age (≤70

vs >70 years) for all outcomes.            In addition, modified Rankin Scale at Day 90, functional

independence at Day 90, recanalization at 24                                                                                                                                                hours, reperfusion at 24 hours, and good

recovery in Glasgow Outcome Score at Day 90 were adjusted for type of occlusion

(ICA/M1 vs M2), and reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT was adjusted for baseline

NIHSS score (≤15 vs >15).

         In Figure 2, age subgroups were adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and

baseline NIHSS (≤15 vs >15); the subgroup with age <80 years was additionally

adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2). NIHSS subgroups were adjusted for

protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and age (≤70 vs >70 years); the NIHSS ≤15 subgroup was

additionally adjusted for type of occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2). Subgroups for time between

stroke onset to randomization were adjusted for protocol version (1-4 vs 5) and type of

occlusion (ICA/M1 vs M2); additionally, the subgroup that had <16 hours between stroke

onset to randomization was adjusted for age (≤70 vs >70 years). Subgroups for planned

          Hypothesis testing of the secondary efficacy outcomes would be performed only

if the primary efficacy analysis was statistically significant. To account for multiplicity,

tests of the secondary outcomes were to be conducted sequentially, as detailed in the

main text above and in study Statistical Analysis Plan, until a negative result indicated by

a P                           value >0.049. P                                                                                                                                                    values were not provided for any subsequent analyses of the

secondary outcomes following a negative test and the 95% CIs should not be used for

hypothesis testing.

          Multiple imputation was conducted as sensitivity analysis for the primary and

several secondary outcomes (i.e., functional independence at 90 days, 24 hours

recanalization, 24 hours reperfusion, and  reperfusion at the conclusion of EVT),

respectively.
