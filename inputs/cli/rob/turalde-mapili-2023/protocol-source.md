# Efficacy and safety of nirsevimab for the prevention of respiratory syncytial virus infection among infants: a systematic review and meta-analysis

## Review objective

To assess the efficacy and safety of nirsevimab for preventing respiratory syncytial virus infection and related illness in infants.

## Review question

In infants aged one year or younger, how does nirsevimab compare with placebo for preventing respiratory syncytial virus infection and related illness, and what are its safety outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Infants aged one year or younger.

### Intervention

Nirsevimab administered alone.

### Comparator

Placebo.

### Study design and report eligibility

Randomized controlled trials published from database inception through June 2022. Reports must be written in English or have an English translation. Nonrandomized studies, studies without a placebo comparator, and studies reporting only outcomes outside the review scope will be excluded.

## Information sources and search strategy

MEDLINE via PubMed, CENTRAL, Scopus, and ClinicalTrials.gov will be searched. The strategy will combine `(nirsevimab OR "MED18897" OR "MED-18897") AND ("Respiratory Syncytial Virus Infection" OR "Infections, Respiratory Syncytial Virus" OR "Human respiratory syncytial virus") AND prevention AND "infants, newborn"`, adapted as required for each source.

## Study selection

Two reviewers will independently remove duplicates, screen titles and abstracts, and assess full texts. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion with two additional reviewers.

## Outcomes

### Primary efficacy outcome

- Respiratory syncytial virus illness.

### Secondary efficacy outcomes

- Hospitalization related to respiratory syncytial virus.
- Any respiratory illness.

### Primary safety outcome

- Life-threatening or fatal adverse events.

### Secondary safety outcomes

- Adverse events of special interest, including hypersensitivity, immune-complex disease, and thrombocytopenia.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract study design, participant characteristics, intervention and control dosing schedules and routes, and event counts for eligible efficacy and safety outcomes. Full articles, appendices, and supplementary files will be used. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion with two additional reviewers until consensus is reached.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Two reviewers will independently assess risk of bias using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion.

## Data synthesis

Analyses will be conducted in Review Manager 5.4. Dichotomous outcomes will be summarized as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals using random-effects models. Heterogeneity will be assessed with I²; values from 40% to 70% will be considered moderate and values above 70% substantial.

## Certainty of evidence

Two reviewers will assess certainty of evidence using GRADE, considering study limitations, directness, consistency, precision, and publication bias.
