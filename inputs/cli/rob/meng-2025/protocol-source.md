# Dexmedetomidine for delirium prevention in adult patients following cardiac surgery: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

## Review objective

To evaluate the efficacy and safety of intravenous dexmedetomidine for preventing postoperative delirium in adults undergoing cardiac surgery.

## Review question

In adults undergoing cardiac surgery, does intravenous dexmedetomidine, compared with placebo or normal saline, reduce postoperative delirium, affect intensive-care-unit length of stay or mortality, or alter the risks of bradycardia and hypotension?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Adults aged 18 years or older undergoing cardiac surgery. Animal studies and studies enrolling participants younger than 18 years will be excluded.

### Intervention

Intravenous dexmedetomidine administered before, during, or after cardiac surgery.

### Comparator

Placebo or normal saline.

### Study design

Randomized controlled trials. Studies using non-intravenous dexmedetomidine, an inappropriate control, or reporting insufficient or non-extractable data will be excluded. Authors may be contacted when required information is missing.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library will be searched for randomized trials published from 1 January 2014 through 10 March 2024. Personal files, reference lists of eligible trials, and relevant meeting reports will also be searched, and trial authors or subject experts may be contacted when necessary.

## Study selection

Searches will be conducted independently by three reviewers. Two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts and then assess full texts against standardized eligibility criteria. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion.

## Outcomes

### Primary outcome

- Incidence of postoperative delirium. Data will be extracted from assessments using the Confusion Assessment Method for the Intensive Care Unit in the cardiac-surgery intensive care unit and the Confusion Assessment Method on the ward; assessments reported at 12-hour intervals will be eligible.

### Secondary outcomes

- Intensive care unit length of stay.
- Mortality.
- Bradycardia.
- Hypotension.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently collect study characteristics, participant characteristics, intervention and comparator details, dexmedetomidine administration timing, and outcome data using a standardized form. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed with the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials.

## Review-method provenance

The published Review selected a fixed-effect model when the heterogeneity-test P value exceeded 0.10 and I² was no more than 50%, and otherwise allowed a random-effects model. This outcome-dependent switching rule is retained only as provenance and does not govern the benchmark analysis.

## Data synthesis

Analyses will be conducted in Review Manager 5.4. Dichotomous outcomes will be summarized as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals and continuous outcomes as mean differences with 95% confidence intervals. All Target meta-analyses will use a random-effects model selected before inspecting Study effect estimates, Q, or I² because the eligible Review combines different cardiac procedures, dexmedetomidine administration timings and dosing regimens, outcome assessment periods, and trial-level outcome definitions. Cochran's Q and I² will describe observed heterogeneity but will not select or change the analysis model. Two-sided P values below 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

## Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

Delirium will be examined by preoperative, intraoperative, or postoperative dexmedetomidine administration. Leave-one-study-out sensitivity analysis will assess the influence of each study on each predefined outcome.
