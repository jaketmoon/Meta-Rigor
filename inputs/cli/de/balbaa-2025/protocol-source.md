# Non-fasting versus fasting before minimally invasive cardiac procedures: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol

## Review objective

To compare the efficacy and safety of non-fasting and fasting protocols in patients undergoing minimally invasive cardiac procedures.

## Review question

In patients undergoing minimally invasive cardiac procedures, how do non-fasting protocols compare with fasting protocols for patient satisfaction, procedural safety, and other eligible clinical outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Patients undergoing minimally invasive cardiac procedures, including percutaneous coronary intervention, transcatheter aortic valve replacement, and catheter ablation.

### Intervention

A non-fasting protocol.

### Comparator

A fasting protocol.

### Study design

Randomized controlled trials reporting at least one eligible outcome.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials will be searched from inception through September 2024.

## Study selection

Records will be managed in Covidence. After duplicate removal, two reviewers will independently screen records and two reviewers will independently assess full texts. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus after consultation with a senior reviewer.

## Outcomes

### Primary outcome

- Composite patient-satisfaction score.

### Secondary outcomes

- Hunger score.
- Thirst score.
- Anxiety score.
- Tiredness or fatigue score.
- Intraoperative or postoperative aspiration.
- Postprocedural pneumonia.
- Emergency endotracheal intubation.
- Nausea or vomiting.
- Anti-emetic use.
- Hypoglycemia, defined as blood glucose at or below 0.7 g/L.
- Participant receipt of intraoperative fluid provision.
- Contrast-induced acute kidney injury.
- Use of inotropes or vasopressors.
- Need for ventilation.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract data using a standardized Microsoft Excel form. Fields will include study design and country, anesthesia modality, fasting and non-fasting protocols, fasting duration, procedure type, primary endpoints, participant numbers and baseline characteristics, and all eligible outcomes. Disagreements will be resolved by a senior reviewer.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Two reviewers will independently assess randomized trials using Cochrane RoB 2 across the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, selection of reported results, and overall bias. Disagreements will be resolved with a senior reviewer.

## Review-method provenance

The published Review selected a random-effects model when the chi-square test or I² indicated significant heterogeneity and otherwise selected a fixed-effect model. This outcome-dependent switching rule is retained only as provenance and does not govern the benchmark analysis.

## Data synthesis

Analyses will be conducted in R 4.3 using the `meta`, `metafor`, and `dmetar` packages. Dichotomous outcomes will be summarized as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals and continuous outcomes as mean differences with 95% confidence intervals. All Target meta-analyses will use a random-effects model selected before inspecting Study effect estimates, Q, or I² because the eligible Review combines different cardiac procedure types, anesthesia modalities, fasting protocols, and trial-reported outcome definitions. Cochran's Q and I² will describe observed heterogeneity but will not select or change the analysis model. A chi-square P value below 0.10 will indicate significant heterogeneity. I² values of 0% to 40% will be considered low, 30% to 60% moderate, 50% to 90% substantial, and 75% to 100% considerable heterogeneity.

## Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

Subgroup analyses will be conducted where sufficient data are available. Sensitivity analyses will examine the influence of studies at high risk of bias or contributing substantially to heterogeneity.
