# Endovascular therapy for acute ischemic stroke with a large ischemic core: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol

## Review objective

To determine whether endovascular therapy plus medical care improves clinical outcomes compared with medical care alone in patients with acute ischemic stroke and a large ischemic core.

## Review question

In patients with acute ischemic stroke and a large ischemic core, does endovascular therapy plus medical care, compared with medical care alone, improve functional and safety outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by internal carotid artery or middle cerebral artery M1 occlusion and a large infarct core, defined as an Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score of 5 or lower on non-contrast CT or diffusion-weighted imaging, or an estimated ischemic-core volume of at least 50 mL on CT perfusion imaging.

### Intervention

Endovascular therapy plus medical care, including intravenous thrombolysis when eligible.

### Comparator

Medical care alone, including intravenous thrombolysis when eligible.

### Study design

Randomized controlled trials reporting eligible clinical outcomes. Registries, case reports, case-control studies, and cohort studies will be excluded.

## Information sources and search strategy

PubMed, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, and ClinicalTrials.gov will be searched from 1 January 2000 through 25 September 2024 without language restrictions. Search concepts will cover acute ischemic stroke, large ischemic core, endovascular treatment, and randomized trials. Citations in retrieved trials will be checked to identify additional eligible trials.

## Outcomes

### Primary outcome

- Functional independence, defined as modified Rankin Scale 0 to 2 at 90 days.

### Secondary outcomes

- Ordinal shift across the full modified Rankin Scale distribution at 90 days.
- Ambulation, defined as modified Rankin Scale 0 to 3 at 90 days.
- Nondisabled status, defined as modified Rankin Scale 0 to 1 at 90 days.
- Early neurological improvement.
- Requiring constant care or death, defined as modified Rankin Scale 5 to 6 at 90 days.
- Death by 90 days.
- Early symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract trial characteristics, participant age and sex, enrollment imaging criteria, interventions, comparators, and eligible outcomes. Disagreements will be resolved by reference to the original report.

## Risk-of-bias assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed using RoB 2 across the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, and selection of the reported result.

## Data synthesis

Analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle. When at least two trials provide sufficient data, inverse-variance random-effects meta-analysis will be conducted in Review Manager 5.4. Binary outcomes will be summarized as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Ordinal modified Rankin Scale shift will be summarized using common odds ratios. Two-sided P values below 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Absolute risk reduction and number needed to treat will be calculated for binary outcomes using an assumed comparator risk. For ordinal outcomes, number needed to treat will be derived using the min-max-mean method. Heterogeneity will be assessed primarily by visual inspection of forest plots, and I² will be interpreted cautiously. Leave-one-trial-out analysis and funnel plots will be used where appropriate.

## Subgroup analysis

The primary outcome will be examined by enrollment imaging modality, comparing non-contrast CT or CT perfusion with MRI-based selection.

## Certainty of evidence

Certainty of evidence will be assessed using GRADE.
