# Dynamic measures of fluid responsiveness to guide resuscitation in patients with sepsis and septic shock: a systematic review and meta-analysis

## Review objective

To determine whether resuscitation guided by dynamic measures of fluid responsiveness, compared with resuscitation not guided by dynamic measures, improves clinical outcomes in adults with sepsis or septic shock.

## Review question

In adults with sepsis or septic shock, does resuscitation guided by dynamic fluid-responsiveness measures, compared with resuscitation not guided by such measures, improve mortality and other eligible clinical outcomes?

## Eligibility criteria

### Population

Adults aged 18 years or older with sepsis or septic shock defined by Sepsis II, Sepsis III, or another explicitly reported prespecified definition.

### Intervention

Fluid resuscitation guided by a dynamic assessment of fluid responsiveness. Eligible methods include passive leg raising, a fluid or mini-fluid challenge, pulse-pressure variation, stroke-volume variation, systolic-pressure variation, inferior-vena-cava distensibility or collapsibility, end-expiratory occlusion, and global end-diastolic index. No restriction will be placed on the monitoring device used to obtain the measure.

### Comparator

Resuscitation not guided by dynamic fluid-responsiveness measures, including standard clinical assessment or early goal-directed therapy.

### Study design

Parallel-group randomized controlled trials.

## Information sources and search strategy

MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL and other randomized-trial databases, and Google Scholar will be searched from inception through 3 February 2025. Reference lists of relevant reviews and eligible trials will be checked. No language restriction will be applied.

## Study selection

Pairs of reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts and then full texts using pretested standardized forms. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or arbitration by the lead author.

## Outcomes

### Primary outcome

- Mortality at 28 days.

### Secondary outcomes

- Intensive care unit mortality.
- All-cause mortality at 90 days.
- Intensive care unit length of stay.
- Hospital length of stay.
- Use of vasopressors.
- Duration of vasopressor therapy.
- Use of invasive mechanical ventilation.
- Duration of invasive mechanical ventilation.
- Use of renal replacement therapy.
- Duration of renal replacement therapy.
- Use of inotropes.
- Trial-defined acute kidney injury.
- Intravenous fluid administered on day 1.
- Cumulative fluid balance on day 3.

## Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract the first author, country, sample size, eligibility criteria, participant age, intervention and control procedures, definition and modality of fluid-responsiveness assessment, and outcome data using a pretested standardized form. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or lead-author arbitration.

## Risk of bias and certainty assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed using a modified Cochrane RoB 2 tool. Certainty of evidence will be assessed using GRADE.

## Data synthesis

Random-effects meta-analysis with inverse-variance weighting will be performed in Review Manager 5.4.1. Dichotomous outcomes will be reported as risk ratios and risk differences with 95% confidence intervals. Continuous outcomes will be reported as mean differences with 95% confidence intervals. When necessary, medians will be converted to means and interquartile ranges to standard deviations using Cochrane-recommended methods. Heterogeneity will be assessed with chi-square, I², and visual inspection of forest plots.

## Subgroup analyses

Where data permit, subgroup analyses will examine risk of bias, the fluid-responsiveness measure, monitoring modality or technology, and illness severity.
