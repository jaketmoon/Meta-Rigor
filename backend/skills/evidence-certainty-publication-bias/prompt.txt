# Evidence Certainty Publication Bias V1

Version: `CORE_GRADE_2025_V1`.

1. `NOT_SERIOUS` means no evidence sufficient to strongly suspect publication bias was found, not that absence of bias has been proven.
2. Generally use `SERIOUS` when known unpublished Studies/outcomes are large enough to change the pooled result, or all/most evidence comes from small industry-sponsored Studies.
3. Interpret funnel asymmetry or statistical tests alongside Study count, search closure, sponsorship, and other mechanisms. Neither `k < 10`, absence of an Egger test, nor a nonsignificant test alone establishes absence of bias.
4. Selective result reporting within a Study belongs to risk of bias; non-publication of an entire Study/outcome belongs here. Avoid double-counting.
5. Core GRADE V1 generally downgrades by only one level for publication bias. Use `VERY_SERIOUS` only when strong, consistent missing-evidence signals from multiple sources could fundamentally change the conclusion.
6. GRADE asks whether available sources justify strong suspicion, not whether absence of bias can be proven. If sources at least report actual search/availability/selection information, and no known missing Study/outcome, selection mechanism, or small-study pattern could change the conclusion, use `NOT_SERIOUS`; still state search limitations in the rationale.
7. Missing registry searches, sponsorship information, funnel/Egger tests, or unpublished-study signals are common diagnostic gaps and cannot alone make the domain `NOT_ASSESSABLE`. Return `NOT_ASSESSABLE` only when there are no source-bound search/availability/selection facts, or sources conflict so severely that even adjacent boundaries of strong suspicion cannot be constrained.

The rationale must explain the publication mechanism, potentially missing content, and its potential impact on conclusions, and cite every input Evidence Fact id.
